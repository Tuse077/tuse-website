Rats in the House or Ceiling? How to Get Rid of Them

Pest control on Tuse starts from ₦20,000. Describe the problem in the app — iRanti helps with a preliminary category and price band before anyone arrives.

The short answer

Rats come in for food, water and shelter, often through gaps around pipes, the roof edge and drains. Remove food sources, store food in sealed containers, and set traps. For a lasting fix the entry points have to be found and sealed, which is where a professional helps.

Why this happens

Rats can squeeze through a gap about the width of a thumb. Ceilings are a favourite because they are warm, dark and rarely disturbed, and rats often get in at the roof edge or where pipes and cables enter. Once inside, they breed quickly.

Poison on its own often leaves rats dying inside the ceiling or walls, causing a strong smell for weeks, and it can harm pets and children. Professionals combine trapping or baiting with finding and blocking the entry points, so new rats cannot move in.

Common causes

  • Food left out, open bins, and pet food
  • Gaps around pipes, cables, the roof edge and doors
  • Clutter in stores, ceilings and compounds
  • Open drains, and bush or rubbish close to the building

What you can safely check

These are low-risk checks only. Do not open live electrical panels, handle refrigerant, or dismantle fuel systems. If anything feels unsafe, stop and book a verified professional.

  1. Look for droppings, gnaw marks, greasy rub marks and nests to see where they are active
  2. Store food, including grains and pet food, in sealed containers
  3. Keep bins closed, and clear rubbish and clutter from the compound
  4. Set traps along walls where you have seen activity, out of reach of children and pets

When to call a professional

  • Gnawed electrical cables, which are a fire risk
  • Rats seen during the day, often a sign of a large population
  • A dead-animal smell from the ceiling or walls

FAQs

How do rats get into the ceiling?

Common routes are gaps at the roof edge and fascia, holes where pipes and cables come in, overhanging trees, and cracks in the walls. Sealing these points is the key to keeping rats out.

Is rat poison safe to use at home?

Rat poison is toxic to children, pets and other animals, and dead rats in the ceiling cause bad smells. If it is used at all, it should be in tamper-resistant bait stations, ideally placed by a professional.

Are rats dangerous to my health?

Rat droppings and urine can spread disease, including Lassa fever, which is carried by some rats in Nigeria. Do not touch droppings with bare hands, and clean affected areas with disinfectant while wearing gloves.
