Generator Overheating? Why It Happens and What to Do

Generator repair on Tuse starts from ₦12,000. Describe the problem in the app — iRanti helps with a preliminary category and price band before anyone arrives.

The short answer

Generators overheat when they cannot get rid of heat fast enough: low oil, poor ventilation, overloading, or a dirty engine and cooling fins. Switch it off, let it cool completely, and fix the cause. Running an overheating generator can seize the engine.

Why this happens

Small air-cooled generators rely on a fan blowing air over metal fins on the engine. The oil also carries heat away and stops moving parts rubbing. When the oil is low or old, or the air cannot flow freely, the engine runs hotter and hotter until the protection switches it off, or the engine is damaged.

Generators are often kept in cages, small rooms or under covers to protect them from theft and rain, which can trap hot exhaust air around the set. Running close to the full rating for hours also produces a lot of heat. Many sets run long hours every day, so overheating is one of the most common reasons for repairs.

Common causes

  • Low or old engine oil
  • Running in an enclosed space, or a cage with poor airflow
  • Overloading with more appliances than the set is rated for
  • Dust and dirt clogging the cooling fins and air intake
  • On liquid-cooled diesel sets, low coolant or a blocked radiator

What you can safely check

These are low-risk checks only. Do not open live electrical panels, handle refrigerant, or dismantle fuel systems. If anything feels unsafe, stop and book a verified professional.

  1. Switch the generator off and let it cool completely before touching it
  2. Once cool, check the oil on the dipstick and top up with the correct grade
  3. Make sure the set has open space on every side, with no cover, tarpaulin or closed room trapping the heat
  4. Reduce the load: list what was running and compare it with the set's rating

When to call a professional

  • Smoke, a burning smell, or oil leaking onto hot parts
  • Knocking or grinding from the engine
  • It overheats again quickly, even with the oil topped up and a light load

FAQs

Can a generator overheat from running too long?

Long running hours alone should not overheat a healthy, well-ventilated set running within its rating. But long hours break down the oil faster, so it needs changing more often. If it overheats after a few hours, check the oil, airflow and load.

Is it safe to pour water on an overheating generator?

No. Cold water on a hot engine can crack metal parts, and water near the alternator and electrics is dangerous. Switch it off and let it cool naturally.
